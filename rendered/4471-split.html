  • parser — the shared parser reads every date month first. A tenant that writes the day first gets day and month swapped: 7 April comes out as 4 July. This is the fix that is queued.
  • hint — booking-v3’s form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at a day-first tenant type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. The parser fix does not touch it: the form has to print the tenant’s order.
  • one-day — the date moves by one day, not by a swap: Tuesday becomes Monday, one client in Lisbon and one in Toronto see different days for the same meeting, an invite arrives before the booking. That is a time zone problem, not a parser problem. The desk had already marked these reports (desk-notes.json) but filed them under parser by tenant.

How they were counted

  • One id, one report: the export repeats some reports across pages (uniqueById).
  • The problem comes from the tenant’s locale and form (causes.ts) and, for a one-day move, from what the report says. Never from intakeTag: intake guessed from the reporter’s browser and disagrees with the tenant on dozens of these reports.
  • Every count above is computed from reports-4471.json at render time.
A matching subject is not the same problem. #4471 holds a dozen different subjects, and five of them show up under all three problems. Merging on subject alone (decision 0001) is how three problems ended up as one ticket marked known.

What to do next

  1. Keep the queued parser fix on the parser ticket. It closes that problem only.
  2. Change booking-v3’s date hint to the tenant’s order.
  3. Find where an invite’s date crosses time zones.
  4. New date reports: place them by tenant and body, then merge into the ticket of their problem.
